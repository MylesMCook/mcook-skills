# Serial Position Effect

Source concept: https://lawsofux.com/serial-position-effect/

## When to load

Load this reference when navigation order, menus, carousels, onboarding steps, lists, pricing features, comparison rows, or action placement.

## Lens

People tend to remember the first and last items in a sequence better than the middle.

## Look for in the design

- critical options buried in the middle of a list
- first and last positions used for low-value or distracting items
- ordered sequences where recall matters

## Critique moves

- Place the most important remembered items at the beginning or end of a sequence.
- Put low-priority items in the middle when order must remain long.
- Use grouping or headings if every item cannot get a strong position.

## Watch-out

Do not reorder items when user expectations, frequency, or alphabetical order matter more than recall.

## Pairs well with

Peak-End Rule, Miller's Law, Choice Overload
