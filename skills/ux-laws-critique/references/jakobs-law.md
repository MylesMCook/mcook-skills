# Jakob's Law

Source concept: https://lawsofux.com/jakobs-law/

## When to load

Load this reference when navigation, controls, ecommerce, forms, search, checkout, account flows, redesigns, or novel interaction models.

## Lens

Users bring expectations from other interfaces and prefer familiar patterns when those patterns support the same job.

## Look for in the design

- custom patterns replacing common controls without a clear payoff
- renamed or relocated actions that users expect elsewhere
- redesigns that change learned behavior all at once

## Critique moves

- Use familiar conventions for common tasks so users can focus on their goal.
- Reserve novelty for places where it materially improves the experience.
- When changing established patterns, provide transition aids, preview, or compatibility.

## Watch-out

Do not cite familiarity to block legitimate innovation; weigh the learning cost against the benefit.
