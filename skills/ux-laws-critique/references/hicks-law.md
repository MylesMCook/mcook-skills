# Hick's Law

Source concept: https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/

## When to load

Load this reference when navigation, menus, onboarding, search entry, pricing, settings, filter panels, action bars, or any moment where quick choice matters.

## Lens

Decision time rises as the number and complexity of choices rises.

## Look for in the design

- too many equally weighted actions at a decision point
- complex choices presented before context
- users asked to choose categories, settings, or paths without guidance

## Critique moves

- Prioritize the likely next action and de-emphasize rare alternatives.
- Break complex decisions into smaller steps only when each step reduces uncertainty.
- Use defaults, recommendations, or progressive onboarding to reduce first-use decisions.

## Watch-out

Do not simplify until meaning disappears; if users need nuance, provide it after the first decision.

## Pairs well with

Choice Overload, Cognitive Load, Occam's Razor
