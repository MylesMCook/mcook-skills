# Aesthetic-Usability Effect

Source concept: https://lawsofux.com/aesthetic-usability-effect/

## When to load

Load this reference when visual polish, first impressions, delightful UI, premium feel, or risk that aesthetics are masking usability problems.

## Lens

A polished visual presentation can make people perceive an interface as easier to use, but beauty can also hide real usability defects.

## Look for in the design

- screens that look attractive but still require effort to understand or complete
- visual styling that may make minor friction feel acceptable in a review
- beautiful states that lack error, empty, loading, or edge-case coverage

## Critique moves

- Separate perceived quality from task success: name which visual choices help confidence and which tasks still need evidence.
- Ask for review/testing of the hardest states, not only the hero screen.
- Preserve the aesthetic strengths while fixing any hidden interaction defects.

## Watch-out

Do not praise visual polish as proof of usability; use it as one lens on trust, tolerance, and perceived quality.

## Pairs well with

Peak-End Rule, Jakob's Law, Occam's Razor
