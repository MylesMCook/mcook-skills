# Style DNA

Load this file when style fidelity matters, when writing prompts, or when an output feels too much like a generic infographic.

## One sentence

Clean white background, sparse hand-drawn product-sketch energy, weird but readable metaphor, deadpan Inkblot doing the work, and a few short American English handwritten notes.

## Must-have traits

- 16:9 horizontal body illustration for an article, newsletter, essay, blog, or Notion doc.
- Pure white background: no beige, warm gray, paper texture, retro paper, gradients, shadows, noise, or complex scenery.
- Black hand-drawn line art: thin, slightly wobbly, not vector-perfect, not thick marker cartoon.
- Large quiet area: main subject around 40%-60% of the canvas; at least 35% blank white space.
- Sparse labels: usually 3-5 labels, max 6. Each label should be 1-3 words.
- One image explains one core action, state, structure, or metaphor.
- The structure should be visible through the scene. Do not write the structure type on the image.

## Color rules

- Black: main line art, Inkblot, key objects, boxes, labels.
- Orange: primary flow, route, arrow, handoff, movement from A to B.
- Red: warning, friction, bug, emotional spike, problem, important result.
- Blue: secondary note, system state, feedback, AI/assistant signal, optional context.

Blue is optional. Use less color than feels necessary.

## US English localization

- Default to American English spelling: `color`, `organize`, `behavior`, `center`, `check-in`.
- Labels should feel like whiteboard notes, not translated captions: `trust gap`, `manual drag`, `good enough`, `handoff`, `evidence pile`.
- Everyday US objects can add flavor when they clarify the metaphor: cardboard boxes, sticky notes, legal pad, mailbox with flag, garage pegboard, diner counter, receipt, highway ramp, laundromat cart, school locker, porch steps, coffee mug.
- Use only one or two cultural objects per image. Do not turn the image into Americana cosplay.
- Do not default to the US flag, bald eagle, Statue of Liberty, Capitol dome, Uncle Sam, red-white-blue bunting, baseball, or cowboy imagery unless the article is specifically about that subject.
- Avoid regional, class, political, racial, or ethnic stereotypes. The style is US-facing, not stereotype-facing.

## Absolutely avoid

- Polished commercial illustration.
- PPT infographic, formal flowchart, course slide, architecture diagram.
- Children’s cartoon, mascot sticker, meme face, kawaii expression.
- Complex app UI, real screenshots, SaaS dashboard mockups.
- Dense text blocks, numbered instructions, or every node explained.
- Top-left titles like `Workflow`, `Roadmap`, `Common Mistakes`, `System Architecture`.
- Background detail, shadows, gradients, texture, or decorative scenery.
- Over-explaining the joke. The reader should understand the structure in about one second.
