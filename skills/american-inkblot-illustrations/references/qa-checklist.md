# QA Checklist

Load this after generation/editing or when deciding whether to regenerate.

## Must pass

- 16:9 horizontal.
- Clean pure white background.
- Inkblot appears.
- Inkblot performs the core conceptual action; it is not decorative.
- The image uses a fresh metaphor for the current article.
- Weird, clever, and memorable, but still readable.
- Clean and sparse; main subject does not exceed about 60% of canvas.
- One image explains one core structure.
- Labels are American English, short, sparse, and readable.
- Orange is only for main path, arrow, route, or handoff.
- Red is only for warning, friction, problem, emphasis, or result.
- Blue is only for secondary note, feedback, AI/system state, or context.
- The output does not lean on patriotic clichés unless the article requires them.

## Failure signals

Regenerate or locally edit when:

- Top-left title appears: `Workflow`, `Roadmap`, `Common Mistakes`, `System Architecture`, etc.
- Inkblot looks like a mascot, sticker, emoji, child cartoon, animal, or human caricature.
- Image resembles a PPT slide, course page, formal flowchart, or architecture diagram.
- Too many nodes, arrows, labels, boxes, UI panels, or explanations.
- Background has paper texture, beige, shadow, gradient, noise, or decorative scenery.
- Text becomes paragraph-length, unreadable, misspelled, or not US English.
- The image is too polished, too cute, too patriotic by default, or too generic.
- The object/composition closely repeats a known source-project example.

## Iteration moves

- Too ordinary: make Inkblot do the weird physical work; add one low-tech object that embodies the metaphor.
- Too complex: remove nodes; keep one action, 1-2 objects, and 3-5 short labels.
- Too cute: specify deadpan, blank serious expression, not cute, not mascot, no shiny eyes.
- Too PPT: remove title, grid, strict boxes, formal arrows, and tidy corporate layout; make it a hand-drawn scene.
- Too text-heavy: remove title/subtitle/sentences; keep only label fragments.
- Too patriotic: remove symbols and keep everyday US editorial objects only if useful.
- Text errors: first try local edit; if errors are widespread, regenerate with fewer labels.

## Delivery bar

A strong image makes the reader think “that is weird” and then understand the article’s structure within about one second. If it reads like a tutorial slide, it fails.
